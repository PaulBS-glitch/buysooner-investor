INVESTMENTTHESIS

BuySooner turns a housing affordability problem into a refinance-led capital cycle.

The proposition is simple: provide a temporary 10% Boost at purchase, charge for the capital and target one refinance that repays both the external Boost financier and BuySooner. If full take-out is not yet available, a two-stage refinance can repay the financier first and BuySooner later.

CUSTOMER FEE ON HOME VALUE

80% LVR

TARGET REFINANCE TAKE-OUT

Y3 / Y4

LENDER / BS LIKELY TAKE-OUT

1. Large structural need

Many aspiring owners have the income trajectory to service a home, but not enough capital at the moment they need it.

2. Short-duration design

BuySooner is designed to exit through refinance, not to remain a long-term co-owner harvesting property appreciation.

3. Risk is managed twice

A senior lender underwrites the borrower and property. BuySooner separately underwrites the exit path and portfolio fit.

REFINANCESTRATEGY

Plan A is a single refinance. Plan B protects capital velocity if full take-out is not yet available.

The objective is to refinance the entire Boost obligation at once. The two-stage pathway is a fallback designed to return external financier capital quickly rather than forcing it to wait for BuySooner fees to become fully refinanceable.

PLAN A

ONE REFINANCE

1. Preferred: full take-out

Once refinance capacity is sufficient, one conventional refinance repays the external Boost financier and BuySooner together. This is the preferred customer and portfolio outcome.

ONE REFINANCEFinancier + BuySooner repaid

PLAN B

TWO STAGES

2. Fallback: lender first

If capacity is sufficient to repay the financier but not the full BuySooner balance, Plan B applies available capacity to the financier first so its capital can be recycled quickly.

CAPITAL VELOCITY

PROTECTED

3. BuySooner exits later

Any residual BuySooner receivable remains secured. A later refinance repays BuySooner as equity, mortgage amortisation and borrower capacity continue to improve.

Why the distinction matters The Year 3 / Year 4 benchmarks in the housing analysis are lender take-out tests, not promises that BuySooner is fully repaid then. Plan A targets full repayment in one refinance; Plan B avoids trapping external capital until BuySooner’s later fees are also refinanceable.

RISKCONTROLS

BuySooner cannot control the housing cycle. It can control what it originates and how the book evolves.

Controls operate at transaction, customer and portfolio level, allowing exposure to tighten as the market changes.

Origination

Every transaction must show a credible refinance pathway under conservative assumptions: serviceability, property quality and liquidity, refinance LVR and portfolio fit. The senior lender separately underwrites borrower and property.

Customer

Existing exposures are monitored through the term. Income, credit conduct, property performance and refinance viability can trigger earlier engagement, active exit planning and a formal Year 3 review.

Portfolio

Geography, property type, contribution mix, vintage, duration and expected exit timing are monitored. BuySooner can tighten limits, restrict markets, increase underwriting or pause originations.

Result: risk can be reduced before it compounds

STRUCTURAL HOUSING THESIS

Population is growing faster than housing supply can adjust.

That imbalance does not eliminate housing cycles. It creates persistent long-run pressure on demand, rents and home values when supply fails to keep pace.

DEMAND

1.5%

annual population growth latest ABS year to Dec 2025

OECD 2026: Australia’s latest 5-year population growth averaged 1.4% p.a. versus 0.5% across the OECD.

SUPPLY

420

dwellings per 1,000 people vs ~467 OECD average

Grattan finds Australia is one of only four OECD countries where housing per person declined over the past two decades.

THE MECHANISM

More people + constrained supply

persistent excess → demand

Unless supply catches up materially or demand growth changes, the structural pressure remains upward.

WHY THIS MATTERS TO BUYSOONER

This structural imbalance is the recovery engine beneath the refinance thesis. BuySooner still stress-tests cyclical falls, but it does not assume a permanently stagnant housing market as the central case.

Appendix 2 / Customer eligibility and exclusions

BuySooner is for mortgage-capable buyers with a defined capital gap.

Eligibility is deliberately narrow: the customer must support the senior mortgage, contribute equity and show a credible refinance pathway, with sale available as a fallback.

01

Mortgage-capable

The customer must pass senior lender assessment and demonstrate capacity to service the first mortgage.

02

Deposit-constrained

BuySooner supports the missing settlement contribution. It does not remove the need for customer capital at risk.

03

Standard residential property

The property should be ordinary, marketable and capable of supporting refinance or sale exit.

04

Exit viable before approval

If a credible refinance pathway cannot be demonstrated under conservative assumptions, the transaction should not proceed. Sale remains a fallback pathway.

Eligibility discipline

The product works because the customer segment is specific.

BuySooner targets a capital-timing gap, not weak-credit lending or general affordability stress.

Customer Borrowing capacity established by senior lender.
Equity Customer capital committed from day one.
Contribution BuySooner contribution inside approved bands.
Exit Refinance pathway tested upfront; sale retained as fallback.

Excluded from core pathway

What BuySooner should not fund.

Weak-credit borrowers, speculative or illiquid property, transactions outside contribution limits, or cases where refinance, sale or controlled exit cannot be credibly demonstrated.

Appendix takeaway: BuySooner is for mortgage-capable, deposit-constrained buyers with customer equity, standard property collateral and a credible exit pathway.

Appendix 3 / How BuySooner evaluates risk

BuySooner evaluates risk through layered underwriting and exit discipline.

The senior lender assesses the first mortgage. BuySooner separately assesses the contribution layer, property, portfolio fit and exit pathway.

01

Senior lender underwriting

The bank or non-bank lender assesses serviceability, credit conduct, borrower capacity and first-ranking security.

02

BuySooner underwriting

BuySooner assesses contribution size, collateral quality, borrower profile, exit pathway and portfolio concentration.

03

Exit viability gate

A transaction is not approved unless a credible refinance pathway can be demonstrated under conservative assumptions. Sale remains a fallback pathway.

04

Review and exit discipline

Year 3 review, valuation update, refinance assessment and exit classification drive the pathway after settlement.

Risk control logic

Risk is controlled through underwriting, limits and review.

BuySooner does not rely on a single approval point. It combines senior lender serviceability, customer equity, contribution limits, property eligibility and exit testing.

Senior lender test

Confirms first mortgage serviceability.

BuySooner assessment

Tests contribution size, property quality and portfolio fit.

Exit discipline

Confirms refinance, partial exit, sale or fallback pathway.

Preferred exit

Refinance first. Sale fallback.

If refinance is not viable, BuySooner uses partial exit, sale or recovery processes under policy.

Investor takeaway: BuySooner risk is assessed through layered underwriting, contribution controls, property eligibility and exit viability testing before approval, with scheduled review and refinance-first execution after settlement.

Appendix 4 / Exit risk and fallback pathways

Exit risk is managed through review, classification and fallback pathways.

BuySooner is not indefinite exposure. Each transaction needs a credible exit pathway before approval and a clear review process before the policy endpoint.

Exit process

Review milestones create a defined path to exit.

Exit tested before approval

→
03

Exit classification

The file is classified into full refinance, partial exit, sale fallback or final resolution.

→
04

Fallback execution

Sale or recovery pathway is used where refinance or controlled partial exit is not viable.

Exit classification

Every review should produce a clear pathway.

Full refinance viable

Full BuySooner repayment can be refinanced.

Partial exit viable

Part repayment can be achieved, with residual exposure managed.

Sale fallback required

Refinance is not viable; sale becomes the primary fallback.

Final resolution

Structured file management where ordinary exit is not achieved.

Control point

Refinance first. Sale fallback.

The exit pathway is reviewed, classified and acted on before the policy endpoint. Clear review milestones prevent indefinite exposure and force escalation where refinance readiness weakens.

Appendix 5 / Portfolio construction and concentration controls

Portfolio risk is controlled by the shape of the pool, not only by individual approvals.

BuySooner monitors concentration by geography, property type, contribution band, origination vintage and expected exit timing, with authority to tighten settings as risk indicators change.

Portfolio control framework

Risk domain → policy focus → trigger → management response

Risk domain Policy focus Dynamic trigger Management response
01Geography & postcode clustering

State, metropolitan area, postcode and local-market concentration.

Exposure begins to accumulate in a narrow property market.

Restrict locations or slow origination.

02Property type & liquidity

Houses, townhouses and apartments; restrict non-standard, illiquid or oversupplied segments.

Property-type concentration rises or liquidity conditions weaken.

Restrict higher-risk property types.

03Contribution band mix

Core Retail, Elevated Retail, Structured and Exception contribution bands.

Higher-contribution files begin to dominate the pool.

Reduce limits or require escalation.

04Vintage & exit timing

Settlement cohorts, Year 3 reviews, Year 5 endpoint risk and refinance timing.

Refinance or sale-exit clustering, or weakening refinance conditions.

Stagger originations, prioritise exits, tighten assumptions or pause marginal deals.

Portfolio discipline

Concentration is an approval and monitoring issue.

The same credit settings that approve transactions must also govern the composition and risk profile of the portfolio. The control is not just reporting: management must be able to tighten criteria before concentration becomes capital risk.

Appendix takeaway: portfolio indicators are designed to change origination behaviour before concentration becomes capital impairment.
